FEATURED RESEARCH

Kinetics and Optimisation of Enzymatic Hydrolysis of Starch from Corn Cob

Bachelor of Engineering Research Project focused on biomass conversion, enzymatic hydrolysis, reaction kinetics and process optimisation using Response Surface Methodology.

📄 View Full Project ⬇ Download PDF ← Back to Projects

Project Overview

This research investigated the enzymatic hydrolysis of starch extracted from corn cob using α-amylase.

Response Surface Methodology (RSM) was employed to optimise enzyme concentration, pH and reaction time in order to maximise glucose yield.

The research also investigated the kinetics of the hydrolysis reaction and compared different kinetic models to determine the model that best described the reaction behaviour.

The study demonstrates the potential of agricultural waste such as corn cobs as a valuable feedstock for starch recovery and subsequent conversion into fermentable sugars.

Research Methodology

The experimental work involved starch extraction from corn cob followed by enzymatic hydrolysis using α-amylase.

A Central Composite Design (CCD) under Response Surface Methodology was used to investigate the effects of three independent variables:

  • Enzyme concentration: 1.0–6.36 mg/mL
  • pH: 4.5–7.5
  • Hydrolysis time: 30–120 minutes

The experimental design consisted of 20 experimental runs. Model fitting, ANOVA, regression analysis, surface plots and contour plots were performed using Design-Expert.

Experiments were conducted in triplicate and statistical significance was evaluated at a 95% confidence level.

Response Surface Methodology & Optimisation

Response Surface Methodology was used to determine the combined effect of enzyme concentration, pH and reaction time on glucose yield.

Statistical modelling identified enzyme concentration and reaction time as important factors influencing glucose production.

Numerical optimisation produced the optimum operating conditions of:

Factor Optimum Condition
Enzyme Concentration 5.596 mg/mL
pH 6.117
Reaction Time 89.667 minutes
Predicted Glucose Yield 99.61%

Validation experiments produced glucose yields of 98.76%, 98.92% and 98.60%, with percentage errors below 1.1%, confirming the reliability of the optimisation model.

Kinetic Analysis

Kinetic analysis was performed under the optimised process conditions using three initial substrate concentrations:

  • 5 mg/mL
  • 10 mg/mL
  • 15 mg/mL

Experimental data were fitted to first-order, second-order and Michaelis–Menten kinetic models.

The Michaelis–Menten model provided the best description of the experimental data with an excellent correlation coefficient of R² = 0.9984.

Research Significance

Corn cobs are an abundant agricultural residue that can serve as a potential source of starch and fermentable sugars.

This research demonstrates how agricultural waste can be converted into value-added products through enzymatic processing.

The optimisation of hydrolysis conditions can improve glucose production while reducing unnecessary enzyme usage and processing time.

The kinetic analysis also provides useful information for understanding and potentially scaling up the enzymatic hydrolysis process.
